# Cairn v0.11.2

`v0.11.2` (`drystane-cairn==0.11.2`) follows v0.11.1. It makes the judging of
contradictions between graph facts more accurate, in two steps, and explains
and relabels the degraded recall seen shortly after a save or a start. The
REST contract, catalogue schema, Attic format, client commands and locked
dependencies are unchanged, and no rebuild is required. This release ships
source and local build recipes, not container images: `CAIRN_IMAGE` and
`CAIRN_EDGE_IMAGE` in `deploy/images.lock` name local source-build tags.
Garden remains Linux only.

Two of the three items listed as known in the v0.11.1 notes are addressed
here; the third is still open.

## Change: a contradiction is confirmed before a graph fact is ended

When a new memory is projected, a model judges whether any fact already in
the graph is contradicted by it, and Graphiti ends the facts so judged. The
judge sometimes declared a contradiction between facts that have nothing to
do with each other.

A declared contradiction is now confirmed before it is applied: one further
call, about that item alone, must agree, and a fact is ended only if both
judgements name it. An item that declares no contradiction costs nothing
extra. A confirming call that fails fails the item, as any call of the judge
would.

Measured on a synthetic corpus of 20 items with six real contradictions among
unrelated facts, restatements and elaborations
(`tests/fixtures/memory_quality/edge-dedup-unrelated-v1.json`), ten repeats,
the runs with and without confirmation being separate samples:

| Path | Wrong retirements, without | Wrong retirements, with | Real contradictions missed of 60, without | With |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Combined call (bulk loading) | 7 | 2 | 6 | 4 |
| Single call | 9 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**Price.** One further call on the medium model for each item that declares
a contradiction. On 472 recorded judgements from ordinary use, two or three
items did.

## Change: the combined judging call reasons at low effort

With `delivery.chunk_size` above 1, Cairn asks the judge about up to ten
items in one call. That call ran with the model's reasoning switched off,
like every other call Cairn makes. It missed real contradictions, and missed
more the more items shared the call: without confirmation, 10 of 180 at ten
items per call, 5 at five, 3 at three and none at one. Smaller calls are not
a remedy, because wrong retirements rose at five and three items and one item
per call is ten times the calls. Confirmation cannot repair a miss either: it
only removes contradictions the first call declared.

That one call now runs with reasoning effort `low`. Extraction, the single
call and the confirming call keep reasoning off. The efforts are constants
beside the pinned models (`src/cairn/projection/models.py`,
`docs/operations/pinned-models.md`), not configuration.

Measured on the same corpus, ten items per call, 30 repeats per arm in each
of two runs:

| Reasoning effort | Missed of 360, no confirmation | Wrong retirements, no confirmation |
|---|---|---|
| none | 16 | 54 |
| low | 3 | 18 |

As released, with confirmation, ten repeats: no real contradiction missed of
60 at low where 3 were missed at none, and no wrong retirement in either.
Two older corpora showed no regression. `scripts/evaluate_edge_dedup.py`
repeats the measurement; its `--combined-reasoning` option measures another
effort.

**Price.** The combined call took about 4.2 s where it took 2.4 s and wrote
about twice the output tokens, half of them reasoning; input is unchanged. A
bulk load of 184 synthetic memories took the same time within run-to-run
spread. That load makes about ten combined calls; a load of dense text makes
many times more, and its duration has not been measured. The default
`delivery.chunk_size` of 1 never issues the combined call and is unaffected.

**Not established.** Every figure in these two sections is from synthetic
one-sentence items; the effect on real memory text has not been measured.
Some misses and wrong retirements remain. As before, none of this changes
what recall returns: graph end dates never hide a memory.

## Fix: degraded recall during a projection no longer calls for a rebuild

The v0.11.1 notes listed, without a cause, that recall had been seen to
answer in its lexical mode with `semantic_degraded: true` shortly after a
start and to recover by itself. The cause is now established. While a memory
is being projected into the graph, semantic search in its partition is
refused, because the index does not yet cover every memory, and recall
answers lexically. This lasts as long as the projection: seconds for a save,
longer for a bulk chunk or a rebuild. After a start it is the memories still
queued for projection.

That behaviour is kept (ruled 23 November 2026): recall does not read around
an index that is being written. What was wrong was the signal. Cairn logged
the warning `semantic_rebuild_needed`, which asks the operator for a rebuild
that is not needed. Such a refusal is now logged as
`semantic_projection_in_flight` at information level. A refusal with no
projection in flight, an index that failed its start-up verification and a
failure inside a projection keep the warning.

The count of projections in flight is per process and not per partition, so
a real coverage defect found while any projection runs is logged as in
flight until a search is refused with nothing running.
`docs/operations/semantic-fact-search.md` describes both events.

## Known and not changed

- If the task that issues a combined judging call is itself cancelled before
  it starts, for example when the event loop shuts down, the items waiting on
  it are not answered by it. Found by reading the code; whether it occurs in
  practice is not established.
- When the graph database goes away during a bulk chunk, the
  `projection_bulk_demoted` log entry does not say so; it carries the same
  code as other adapter failures.

Both are open issues.
